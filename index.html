The gap

Strong theory, little scalable measurement

Engagement is hand-coded

L2 feedback-engagement studies rely on small samples and manual coding.

Revisions aren't linked to dialogue

No fine-grained method ties AI feedback turns to the revisions they shape.

Peer vs. AI is rarely compared

Few studies compare both sources within the same learners.

Guiding questions

Three questions drive the project

Q1 · SCAFFOLDING

How should AI scaffold?

Concept explanations, strategy hints and metacognitive prompts matched to each student's difficulty, so they keep reasoning on their own.

Adaptive scaffolding
Q2 · INTERACTION

How do students interact with AI?

What they ask, what help they seek, how the AI responds, and what those patterns reveal about learning needs.

This master's study

Three research questions

The study builds the measurement layer the guiding questions depend on.

RQ1 · MEASUREMENT

Can an LLM code engagement reliably?

Compared with trained human coders, category by category.

RQ2 · METHOD

How does dialogue become revision?

Which comments get taken up, and whether the writing improves.

RQ3 · EMPIRICAL

Peer or AI: what changes?

Same learners, same draft, two feedback partners.

Study design

One draft, two partners, one revision

Feedback order is counterbalanced. About 2–3 lessons.

STEP 1

Draft

A task from the existing curriculum.

STEP 2

Two dialogues

Peer · classmate
AI · feedback assistant
STEP 3

Revise once

Take up, adapt, or reject.

STEP 4

Reflect

Five minutes: what changed, and why.

Contributions

What this project adds

MEASUREMENT

A validated engagement codebook

Automated coding of interaction logs, tested for transfer from university EFL to secondary learners.

METHOD

Alignment for dialogic feedback

Feedback turn to revision, copying vs. adapting. Beyond n-gram overlap and one-way systems like eRevise.

EMPIRICAL

Peer vs. AI, same learners

A direct human–human vs. human–AI comparison on one draft.

Next phase

Adaptive scaffolding that fades

A learner model picks the level of help. The LLM only words it. Try the rule below.

L0
No intervention
L1
Metacognitive prompt · “Does each sentence support your claim?”
L2
Locate · “Look at sentences 2 and 3.”
L3
Strategy hint · “Explain how the evidence proves it.”
L4
Concept explanation or contrasting examples
L5
Modelled revision, then explain it
Help types concept · strategy · metacognitiveStep up after weak uptakeFade after 3 accurate explanations

Planned test: adaptive vs. fixed vs. no scaffolding · outcomes: revision quality, transfer, independence
